  3-4 samples at a "nominal" pose — camera roughly straight down at the marker, just translating the arm slightly between shots (small XY moves, tag comfortably in frame, not too oblique). This anchors the easy case.
~4 samples rotating about the camera's roll axis (rotate the wrist/hand around its own viewing axis) — e.g. 0°, ±30°, ±60° relative to nominal, while still looking generally at the marker.
~4 samples tilting the camera off-axis (pitch/yaw) — angle the camera in toward the marker from different oblique directions (left side, right side, front, back), each maybe 30-45° off vertical. This is what gives you the "left/right" diversity you were missing.
~4-6 samples specifically bridging toward your problem orientation — since your real failure shows up at a ~180° wrist flip, don't jump straight from nominal to fully flipped. Take intermediate samples at roughly 45°, 90°, 135°, and then as close to your actual 180°-flip operating pose as still keeps the marker trackable. If the flipped orientation genuinely can't see the marker at all from any position, that's important information too — it means that orientation is fundamentally outside what eye-in-hand calibration (with a single fixed marker) can constrain, and you'd need a marker reachable from that orientation as well, or a second calibration marker position.
A couple of "redundant" repeats at some already-sampled orientation (their doc explicitly recommends redundant poses) — helps average out noise.
